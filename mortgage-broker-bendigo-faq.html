Cost and Payment Questions

The most common opening question is cost. The published guide states the introduction carries no charge and is obligation-free. It also explains the standard payment structure: Australian residential brokers are typically paid commission by the lender rather than by the borrower. Where that arrangement does not apply, any exception or direct fee must be disclosed upfront, in line with ASIC rules.

That structure makes three follow-up questions worth asking before any application is lodged. First, which lenders appear on the comparison and which were excluded. Second, whether any direct fee applies to the borrower in this specific case. Third, whether recommendations are shaped by the commission structures of the lenders being compared. Readers who want a checklist of warning signs alongside these payment questions can use the borrower red flag checklist for the same topic family. Asking early keeps the conversation evidence-led rather than product-led.

Deposit, Guarantees and Scheme Eligibility

Deposit questions dominate for first home buyers. The source guide states eligible first home buyers may purchase with as little as a 5 percent deposit without lenders mortgage insurance under the federal First Home Guarantee, administered by Housing Australia. It also refers to the Victorian First Home Owner Grant for eligible new home purchases. Both settings carry conditions, and price caps are reviewed annually.

The useful question is therefore not whether a scheme exists in general, but whether it fits the intended purchase. Borrowers should confirm current eligibility, the current price caps, and whether the property, established or new, qualifies for the treatment assumed. A 5 percent deposit path should be treated as conditional, not automatic, until a lender assesses the full file against its own policy.

Pre-Approval and Settlement Timing

Two timing anchors appear throughout the guide. Pre-approval, also called conditional approval, is typically valid for around 90 days depending on the lender. Application to settlement commonly takes around four to six weeks, depending on lender and borrower circumstances. Both figures are planning ranges rather than guarantees, and either can move if the file changes.

Pre-approval indicates what a lender is likely to lend before a property is found. It is not the same as formal approval after a purchase contract is in place. Between those stages, updated income evidence, changed debts, a different property or a lender valuation can shift the outcome. Borrowers should ask what could change the file between conditional and formal approval, and what documents would refresh pre-approval if it expires before a property is secured.

Documents and Application Readiness

Before lenders are compared, a clean file helps the broker test policy fit quickly. The guide points to identity details, income evidence, existing loan information, savings history, credit commitments and the intended use of the loan as the core preparation. Stating whether the purchase is owner-occupied or investment, whether the property is established or new, and whether settlement timing is already known sharpens the comparison further.

For local borrowers, scenarios across Kangaroo Flat, Strathdale, Golden Square, Epsom and Eaglehawk show how purpose changes the questions. A first purchase, a refinance and an investment step each pull different lender policies, features such as offset accounts, and repayment types such as principal and interest or interest-only into focus, so the file should match the goal being tested.

  1. Define the loan purpose. State whether the goal is a first purchase, a refinance, an investment step or pre-approval before comparing lenders.
  2. Prepare the file. Gather identity details, income evidence, existing loan information, savings history and credit commitments.
  3. Test policy fit. Ask which lenders suit the deposit, property purpose, repayment preference and timing.
  4. Check payment and features. Confirm how the broker is paid, whether any direct fee applies, and which features such as an offset account are included.
